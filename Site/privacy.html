Privacy policy

Hereabouts for iPhone. Last updated 7 November 2026.

Hereabouts doesn't collect any data. Everything you add stays on your iPhone, apart from the backups you choose to make or turn on, which go to places you control. There is no account, no server, no analytics and no advertising.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Places, homes, things, photos, notes and the history of where things have been.
  • Who you've lent a thing to, and the date you asked for it back.
  • Packing kits, trips and checklists.

Your iPhone's own backup to iCloud or a computer may include this data, under Apple's terms.

Homes, private things and lending

You can keep more than one home in the notebook. A thing marked private is left out of Spotlight, and Siri never says its place out loud. Its place is shown only inside the app. When you lend a thing, you type a name or choose one from Contacts. Apple's contact picker runs outside Hereabouts, so Hereabouts gets no access to your contacts and keeps only the name you choose, in your notebook.

Camera and photos

Hereabouts uses the camera or a photo you choose to picture a thing, a place or a box label. If you use the shelf photo to suggest names for things, the photo is read on your iPhone, on iPhones that support it, and names are suggested for you to confirm. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the suggestions are not saved unless you save them. Box labels are made on your iPhone and printed or shared only when you choose.

Voice memos

If you hold the microphone on Find or tap Say it on the widget, what you say is turned into text on your iPhone with Apple's speech recognition. Hereabouts doesn't keep the recording, only the text you see and choose to save. Understanding what you said uses rules in the app and, on iPhones that support it, Apple's on-device model. The first time, your iPhone may need to download its speech model. Voice memos work in English only for now.

Notifications

Reminders are scheduled on your iPhone. By default they include the thing's name, which you can turn off in Settings. Hereabouts sends a reminder when a lent thing's ask-back date comes, a recap on Sunday evenings and a Still there? check once a month, which you can turn off in Settings, and a reminder the evening before a trip if something isn't packed yet. If you start a free trial of Hereabouts Pro, it can also remind you before the trial ends. Hereabouts doesn't ask for access to your calendar.

Siri, Shortcuts, Spotlight and the widget

The names of your things and their places are added to your iPhone's Spotlight index, so you can find them by searching from the Home Screen. The index stays on your iPhone. When you ask Siri or run a shortcut, Apple handles your request under its own privacy terms, and Hereabouts answers from the notebook on your iPhone. The widget's Find and Say it buttons only open the app. They read nothing from your notebook.

Backups and exports

You can export a backup file or a spreadsheet. "Back up everything" carries only the photos you tick, and a restore replaces the whole notebook. These files go only where you save or share them. They aren't encrypted, so keep them somewhere private.

With Hereabouts Pro, the app makes a backup each week and saves it to your own iCloud Drive, or to your iPhone if iCloud Drive is off, keeping the last five. These backups are stored in your iCloud account, under Apple's terms. The developer can't see them.

Purchases

Hereabouts Pro is bought through Apple. Apple handles payment. StoreKit tells Hereabouts which plans you own, whether you're in a free trial, and when your plan renews or ends, so the app can unlock Pro and show the right dates. Hereabouts never sees your name, payment details or Apple Account.

Deleting your data

Everything is on your iPhone, so you can delete it there. Use erase in Settings, or delete the app. Backups you've saved to iCloud Drive, your iPhone or elsewhere stay until you delete those files. The developer holds none of your data, so there is nothing to ask us to remove.

Children

Hereabouts doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Support emails

If you email support, we see your address and what you write, and use them only to reply. Don't send anything private, such as photos of your belongings, unless you want to.

Changes and contact
